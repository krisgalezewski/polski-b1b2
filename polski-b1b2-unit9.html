Unit 9 › Grammar Focus
Grammar Unit G8 · Part 1
Passive Voice, Cause & Effect, Numerals
Strona Bierna, Przyczynowość, Liczebniki
Three tools that are indispensable for formal Polish — especially in writing about science, society and current affairs. The passive removes the agent; cause-effect connectors build argument; numerals express precision.
Passive Voice — Strona Bierna
Polish has two passive constructions. The choice between them mirrors the aspect distinction: the być-passive describes a state or ongoing process; the zostać-passive describes a completed event.
1. State / ongoing passive — być + past passive participle (imperfective)

Polska jest uznawana za jeden z najcenniejszych rezerwuarów przyrody.

Poland is considered one of the most precious nature reserves. (ongoing state)

Lasy są chronione przez prawo unijne.

Forests are protected by EU law. (ongoing process)

Energia jest produkowana przez farmy wiatrowe.

Energy is produced by wind farms. (continuous production)

2. Event passive — zostać + past passive participle (perfective)

Trzy miliony drzew zostały posadzone w ramach programu zalesiania.

Three million trees were planted as part of the afforestation programme. (completed event)

Kilkadziesiąt rzek zostało zanieczyszczonych.

Dozens of rivers were polluted / became polluted. (completed past event)

Ustawa zostanie przyjęta w przyszłym roku.

The law will be adopted next year. (future completed event)

Note: in Polish, the agent in passive sentences is often omitted entirely — this is much more common than in English.

Large Numerals — Liczebniki wielkie
Large numerals in Polish require careful case agreement. The numeral governs the case of the noun that follows it — this differs from English and is one of the trickiest areas of Polish grammar.
Numeral case rules — noun after the numeral
NumeralNoun caseExample
1 (jeden/jedna/jedno)Nominative sg.jeden park, jedna ustawa
2, 3, 4Nominative pl.dwa parki, trzy lasy, cztery rzeki
