Grammar Unit G2
Genitive & Dative Cases
Dopełniacz i Celownik
These two cases appear constantly in Polish — especially in family and relationship language. Study each rule, then test yourself in the exercises that follow.
The Genitive Case — Dopełniacz
The Genitive answers the questions kogo? czego? (of whom? of what?). It is the most frequently used case after Nominative and Accusative, and has several distinct functions.
1. Possession — "of" / "belonging to"

To jest dom mojej mamy. — This is my mother's house.

Numer telefonu brata — my brother's phone number

The Genitive replaces English "'s" and "of".

2. Absence & Negation — "there is no…" / "I don't have…"

Nie mam siostry. — I don't have a sister.

Nie ma ojca w domu. — Father isn't home.

After negation, the direct object shifts from Accusative → Genitive. This is one of the most common B1 errors.

3. After "brakować" — "to miss / to lack"

Brakuje mi siostry. — I miss my sister. (lit. "of-sister lacks to-me")

Brakuje mi czasu. — I don't have enough time.

The thing missed/lacking takes the Genitive. The person who misses it takes the Dative.

4. After quantities & numbers (2–4 and above)

Mam dwóch braci. — I have two brothers. (Gen. pl.)

Dużo rodziny — a lot of family

Numbers 2–4 use Gen. singular; 5+ and quantities use Gen. plural.

5. After specific prepositions

do domu (to home) · z rodziny (from the family) · od mamy (from mum) · dla siostry (for sister) · bez wsparcia (without support) · u babci (at grandma's)

The Dative Case — Celownik
The Dative answers komu? czemu? (to whom? for what?). It marks the indirect object — the person who receives, benefits, or is affected. It is also required by a group of very common verbs.
1. Indirect object — giving, showing, telling

Dałam siostrze prezent. — I gave my sister a gift.

Powiedz mamie, że zadzwonię. — Tell mum I'll call.

2. "podobać się" — to like / to be attracted to

Podoba mi się jego spokój. — I like his calmness.

Podoba jej się Karol. — She likes Karol.

The person who likes something takes Dative pronouns: mi, ci, mu, jej, nam, wam, im.

3. "brakować" — the person who misses takes Dative

Brakuje mi siostry. — I miss my sister. (mi = Dative of ja)

Brakuje jej rodziców. — She misses her parents.

4. "ufać" — to trust

Ufam ci. — I trust you.

Ufam mojej siostrze. — I trust my sister.

5. "pomagać" — to help; "dziękować" — to thank

Pomagam mamie. — I help mum.

Dziękuję ci. — Thank you (to you).

Dative personal pronouns
ja → mi / mnie ty → ci / tobie on → mu / jemu ona → jej my → nam wy → wam oni/one → im
