Verbal Aspect
Aspekt Czasownika — Imperfective vs Perfective
Verbal aspect is the most important — and most challenging — grammatical concept for English speakers learning Polish. Unlike English tense (which tells you when), Polish aspect tells you how an action is viewed: as an ongoing process or as a completed whole.
The Core Distinction
Every Polish verb belongs to one of two aspects. Most verbs come in pairs — one imperfective, one perfective — with the same basic meaning but different perspective.
Imperfective (niedokonany)

Views the action as ongoing, repeated, habitual, or in progress. Doesn't say whether it finished.

Czytałam książkę. — I was reading a book.

Codziennie gotowała obiad. — She cooked lunch every day.

Piszę list. — I am writing a letter.

Perfective (dokonany)

Views the action as completed, with a result, a single specific event.

Przeczytałam książkę. — I read (finished) the book.

Ugotowała obiad. — She cooked lunch (and it's done).

Napiszę list. — I will write the letter (and finish it).

Key insight: The same past event can be described with either aspect — but the meaning changes. Pisałam (impf.) = I was writing / I wrote (process). Napisałam (pf.) = I wrote (and finished it). The difference is perspective, not time.

Impersonal Passive — świętuje się, obchodzi się
Polish uses the impersonal reflexive construction verb + się to describe customs, habits, and cultural practices without naming a subject. The imperfective aspect is used because these are ongoing or repeated actions.
Structure: imperfective verb + się

W Polsce świętuje się Wigilię 24 grudnia. — In Poland, Christmas Eve is celebrated on 24 December.

Obchodzi się wiele tradycyjnych świąt. — Many traditional holidays are observed.

Przygotowuje się dwanaście potraw. — Twelve dishes are prepared.

Zasiada się do stołu po pierwszej gwiazdce. — One sits down to the table after the first star appears.

Note: the verb agrees with no subject — it stays 3rd person singular. Very common in cultural and journalistic writing.
